Seven findings that should drive the decision

1. The PMS decision and the process-layer decision are independent — do not couple them

Both LeadSimple and Aptly integrate with Rent Manager and AppFolio. Whichever process layer you pick survives a later PMS migration intact. That means you do not have to answer "AppFolio or not" before you answer "LeadSimple or Aptly" — and you should not try to do both at once. Simultaneously replacing your system of record and installing a new operating layer is the single most common way these projects blow up: when something breaks you cannot tell which change caused it.

2. At 300 doors you have just cleared almost every vendor's minimum — this is the cheapest moment to buy

Nearly all of these vendors charge max(rate × doors, monthly minimum), and you are sitting right at the crossover:

  • LeadSimple Platform's $500 minimum binds up to ~168 doors. You are past it.
  • AppFolio Core's ~$298 minimum binds up to ~200 units. Past it.
  • Haven AI's $298 minimum binds up to 298 doors. You are exactly at it.
  • Property Meld's $160 minimum binds up to 100 doors. Past it.
  • AppFolio Plus at $3.20/unit × 300 = $960 — precisely the reported minimum, meaning at your current size you would capture zero benefit from the per-unit rate.

The consequence is the important part: your per-door software cost is essentially flat from 300 to 1,000 doors. There is no volume discount coming and no minimum-fee drag left to absorb. Software is not where your per-door economics change.

3. Payroll is 4–6× the entire software decision, so decide on capability, not price

One $65,000 ops hire, loaded at 28% for taxes and benefits, is $83,200/yr — $23.11 per door per month at 300 doors. Your entire software stack in any scenario above runs roughly $4–7 per door. The whole Rent-Manager-vs-AppFolio, LeadSimple-vs-Aptly debate spans about $3 per door, or a quarter of one employee.

So the real test is not "which is cheaper." It is "which one lets me go from 300 to 500 doors without adding a head." Hold headcount flat across that growth and per-door payroll falls from ~$23 to ~$14 — a $9/door improvement, three times the size of the entire software spread. Set the doors-per-employee input above to see it.

4. LeadSimple and Aptly are not really competing for the same slot

LeadSimple is the consolidation play. At $2.99/door/mo annual, Platform bundles CRM, Phone + Inbox Pro, Operations, Enhanced Maintenance, an AI engine, and unlimited calling and texting. It retires four of your current line items at once: rmVoIP, HubSpot, DocuSign, and the task-manager gap you are trying to fill. Its net cost is materially lower than its sticker — toggle Scenario A above and compare against Today.

One number to brace for. On the baseline assumptions in the model — Rent Manager at $1.50/unit, six seats each on rmVoIP, DocuSign and HubSpot — Option A lands at $4.74 per door against today’s $5.15. That is roughly $123 a month, a rounding error at your size. The honest read: consolidating on LeadSimple is approximately cost-neutral at 300 doors. You are not buying savings — you are getting a workflow engine, an AI engine, a phone system and the task manager you went looking for in the first place for about what you already spend, by retiring three contracts to fund one. Whether that flips into a genuine saving turns entirely on your real HubSpot, DocuSign and rmVoIP invoices, which is precisely why Phase 1 is a baseline exercise and not a formality.

Aptly is the AI-layer play. Its five modules are AI Agents, Leasing, Knowledge, Workflows, and Help Desk, deliberately layered on top of your existing system of record. To answer your question directly: I found no phone system and no e-signature in Aptly's platform documentation. Pick Aptly and you keep paying for rmVoIP and DocuSign, so your contract count does not drop — you are buying capability, not consolidation. Aptly also publishes no pricing at all; it is demo-and-quote only, and the $40/$80-per-user figures floating around third-party directories are unverified.

5. AppFolio's free year is the highest-leverage and highest-risk item on the table

First, a number to pin down. "$329" reads two ways, and they are not close:

  • $329/month flat = $1.10/door at 300 doors — cheaper than almost anything here.
  • $3.29/unit/month = $987/month at 300 doors, $3,290 at 1,000.

Given AppFolio Plus publishes at roughly $3.20/unit, the quote is almost certainly $3.29 per unit per month. The gap between the two readings is about $7,900/year today and $35,500/year at 1,000 doors. Confirm this in writing before anything else.

Second, a free year of subscription is not a free migration. At 300 doors, moving your system of record is realistically 100–200 hours of internal staff time; at a $40/hr loaded rate that is $4,000–8,000 of payroll — comparable to the value of the free year itself, before you count a single reconciliation error. The offer is real value, but it is priced against your team's time, not against zero.

6. Buy the AI agents last, and probably only one

Every path already includes AI: LeadSimple Platform ships an AI engine and an AI voice agent, AppFolio Plus gates Realm-X and its autonomous "AI Performers," and Rent Manager has Orion. Layering Haven ($1/door, $298 min) or Vendoroo (~$3/door) on top before you have measured what is still manual means paying twice for the same capability.

EliseAI is worth ruling out explicitly at your size: reported at $3–6/unit with a ~$25,000 annual minimum, which at 300 doors works out to $6.94/door/month regardless of usage. It is priced for 600+ door multifamily operators. Colleen AI is no longer buyable standalone — Entrata acquired it in June 2024 and folded it into ELI+.

7. Two of your vendors could not be verified, and one dependency is hiding in your own systems

"Get Alvin" — no property management vendor by that name is findable. The closest match to what you described is Avery (averyiq.com), a Y Combinator-backed AI contact center for residential PM that answers calls, texts and emails, schedules tours, opens work orders and coordinates vendors; it reports handling 88% of interactions without a human. Confirm whether Avery is the company you meant.

"Savant" — no Rent Manager email-to-notes integration by that name is findable either. The product matching your description precisely is Email2CRM by Aargh Software, which creates history, notes and service issues in Rent Manager from Outlook or Gmail, attachments included. Worth confirming what you are actually paying for, because both LeadSimple's and Aptly's unified inbox may make it redundant.

The hidden dependency: your NYC developer lead-generation pipeline writes qualified leads into HubSpot with custom properties, per current-status.md in this repo. Both consolidation paths assume you drop HubSpot. Before you cancel it, confirm the replacement CRM accepts programmatic lead creation with custom fields via API or Zapier — otherwise you retire the destination your Supabase pipeline is pointed at. The cheap answer is to keep HubSpot's free tier as the lead-gen landing zone and run the PM sales pipeline in LeadSimple.

Get these in writing before you sign anything

AppFolio

  • Which tier the quote covers — Core, Plus, or Max.
  • The per-unit rate and the monthly minimum after the free year. Reported Plus minimums range from $960 to $1,500; that spread is $6,480/yr.
  • A renewal price cap, in writing, for years 2–3.
  • Whether onboarding and data migration are waived (list price reported at $400–1,000, but expect materially more at 300 doors).
  • What bills separately during the free year — payment processing is reported at $1/unit/mo or $2.49/resident transaction, plus screening and e-sign.
  • Exactly what data migrates: full ledger history, tenant notes, work order history, call recordings, attachments.

LeadSimple

  • Is e-signature native or an integration requiring a third-party signing account? It was announced February 2026 as an integration on phased rollout — if it wraps a provider, you may not actually drop DocuSign.
  • Platform's $500 minimum is described as an annual contract minimum. Confirm the commitment length and the monthly floor.
  • Included AI voice minutes and what overages cost once you exceed them.
  • Whether Operations triggers reliably off Rent Manager data, or needs a middleware layer.
  • Process consulting is quoted at $3,000–9,000 and is not in the per-door price. Decide whether you need it.

Rent Manager

  • Your actual current invoice, line-itemized — you cannot price what you are dropping without it. Rent Manager went quote-only in 2026; historical tiers were $1.00 / $1.50 / $2.50 per unit.
  • rmVoIP's real per-seat cost, broken out separately.
  • What happens to historical call recordings tied to contact records if you leave rmVoIP.
  • What Orion AI costs and which tier gates it.

DocuSign — a live overage risk

Business Pro is $40/user/mo annual and includes only 100 envelopes per user per year, with no rollover. At 300 doors, leases plus renewals plus owner agreements plus vendor paperwork will breach that on a small seat count, and overage envelopes are billed on top. Pull your actual envelope count for the last 12 months before comparing anything — your real DocuSign cost may be well above the $40 line.

The sequence

Ordered by dependency — each phase produces the input the next one needs

These phases are genuinely sequential: Phase 4 cannot be decided well without Phase 3's data, and Phase 5's spend cannot be targeted without Phase 3's measurements.

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–2

Establish your real baseline

Nothing gets decided until these numbers exist

You cannot compute a saving against an unknown. Pull, in one document:

  • Line-itemized current spend: Rent Manager, rmVoIP, the email integration, DocuSign, HubSpot — and your seat count on each.
  • DocuSign envelope volume, trailing 12 months.
  • Inbound call, email and text volume per week, and roughly what share is maintenance vs. leasing vs. accounting. This becomes the ROI denominator for every AI vendor later.
  • Confirm the two unverified vendors: is "Alvin" actually Avery, and is "Savant" actually Email2CRM?
  • Written quotes: LeadSimple Platform at 300 doors annual, Aptly custom quote, AppFolio with all six items from the checklist above, Rent Manager renewal with rmVoIP broken out.
Phase 2 · Weeks 3–4

Choose the process layer — LeadSimple or Aptly

Decided on capability, with price as the tiebreak

Demo both against the same three real workflows, not a generic tour. Use: (a) onboarding a new building, (b) maintenance intake through vendor dispatch to close, (c) a lease renewal 120 days out. Score each on four things:

  • Does it trigger automatically off Rent Manager data, or does someone have to start it by hand?
  • Can a non-technical office person edit a workflow without a consultant?
  • Can it carry an NYC-specific compliance calendar — HPD registration, Local Law inspections, boiler and elevator filings, lead paint?
  • How many of your current contracts does it actually let you cancel?

On current evidence, if consolidation is the goal LeadSimple wins on column coverage; if AI depth on top of an unchanged stack is the goal, Aptly is the sharper tool.

Phase 3 · Weeks 5–10

Install the process layer on Rent Manager — do not migrate yet

The cheap, reversible, high-ROI move
  • Build only the three workflows from Phase 2 first. Resist building twenty; unfinished workflows are worse than none.
  • Run rmVoIP and the new phone system in parallel for 60 days before you cancel anything. Port numbers last.
  • Keep DocuSign until you have personally sent and completed a real lease through the replacement.
  • Measure weekly: manual touches per work order, speed-to-lead on new inquiries, renewal capture rate, and hours per new building onboarded.
Phase 4 · Month 4

Now decide AppFolio, with evidence

The decision Phase 3 makes answerable

After ten weeks on a real process layer you will know something you cannot know today: whether your pain was Rent Manager or your processes. If it was processes, the migration may be unnecessary — and you have saved the free year for a moment when you actually want it. These offers recur; the disruption of a badly-timed migration does not un-happen.

If you do migrate, do it in the accounting off-season, never at year-end close, and budget the 100–200 internal hours explicitly as a real cost.

Phase 5 · Month 5 onward

Buy AI agents against measured gaps, one at a time

Targeted by Phase 3's volume data
  • Take the single highest-volume category still being handled manually and buy one specialist against it — Haven for voice intake at $1/door, Property Meld at $1.60/door if maintenance is the bottleneck, Avery if it is the phone line generally.
  • Write the kill criterion before the pilot starts: the specific percentage of that category handled without a human at 90 days, below which you cancel.
  • One vendor, one workflow, one quarter. Never two AI pilots at once — you will not be able to attribute the result.
  • Skip EliseAI until you are past ~600 doors; its annual minimum makes it the most expensive option per door at your size.

What I need from you to tighten this

Five things would materially change the numbers above, and I have modeled assumptions in their place:

  • Is the AppFolio quote $329/month or $3.29/unit/month? Modeled as $3.29/unit. This is the largest single uncertainty on the page.
  • Your current office headcount and salaries. The payroll panel is seeded with an example roster — two office staff and a bookkeeper — because payroll drives four to six times more per-door cost than software does, so these are the numbers most worth getting right.
  • Seat counts on rmVoIP, DocuSign and HubSpot, plus what you actually pay Rent Manager. Modeled at 6 seats and Rent Manager's historical $1.50/unit Plus tier.
  • Your average management fee per door. Modeled at $85/door/month for the margin line. NYC multifamily on 10+ unit buildings varies widely, and if you bill a flat per-unit fee you will know this figure exactly.
