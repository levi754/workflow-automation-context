The one thing blocking all of it

You asked for reminders that go only to the units that have hose bibs, and only to the buildings that have drains. That sentence contains the whole problem: no reminder tool can target a hose bib until some field somewhere says which units have one. This is a data problem wearing a software problem's clothing, and buying another tool will not solve it.

The good news is genuinely good, and it is the most useful thing I found all session:

You already own the engine. It is switched off.

Rent Manager has User Defined Fields — arbitrary custom fields you can attach to properties and units to track anything the software doesn't already hold. It also has Task Automation, which will "post recurring service issues without any user intervention" once posting schedules are in place. And rmAppSuite Pro can surface selected user-defined fields to maintenance techs in the field.

Those three features are, together, exactly the system you described wanting. The asset registry is the UDFs. The weekly, quarterly, biannual and annual triggers are Task Automation. The delivery to the person holding a screwdriver is rmAppSuite Pro. Incremental cost: $0.

So the build order is: registry first, triggers second, tools last. Spend the next two weeks populating fields, not evaluating vendors. Every recurring ambition on your list becomes mechanical the moment the registry exists, and stays impossible until it does.

Two cautions on this table

Weekly filter reminders will burn out. You asked for weekly and it is specced weekly, but manufacturers generally call for monthly rinsing on washable filters and one to three months on disposables. A weekly resident-facing message is roughly 52 touches a year for a task that needs twelve, and the predictable result is that residents mute the channel — which then also mutes the messages that matter. The version I would ship: weekly for the first month after move-in while the habit forms, then monthly thereafter, with the weekly cadence reserved for units that have had a documented coil freeze or airflow complaint. Your call — the field filter_reminder_cadence lets you run both and compare.

Do not take the statutory dates from this page. Applicability turns on square footage, occupancy type, building age, unit count, stories, mechanical systems and community district. A generic calendar is how buildings get fined. Compute the per-building version with the tool in the next section, and confirm anything with a penalty attached with counsel — missing LL152 alone runs $5,000 per building per BIN. Also worth checking today: HPD registration renews annually, and the deadline is early September.

Is Vendoroo redundant?

Against Avery, Haven or Super — yes, heavily. All four take an inbound maintenance request, triage it, and dispatch a vendor. Buy two and you are paying twice for one capability and splitting your work-order history across two systems, which is worse than either alone.

Against Property Meld — no. Vendoroo is reactive (a request arrives, it handles it); Property Meld also does scheduled preventive maintenance with an asset register. Different halves of the job. Notably Vendoroo's own product pages say nothing about preventive or recurring scheduling — so Vendoroo will not fire your HVAC filter reminders. Don't buy it expecting that.

Can "Alvin" migrate your data? Almost certainly not

I found no evidence that any AI agent vendor in this category performs PMS data migration, and it would be a strange thing for a contact-center product to do. Migrating a ledger means moving chart of accounts, historical transactions, security deposits, prepaids, leases, owner statements and tax history with reconciliation at both ends — that is an accounting data project with legal exposure, not a task for a conversational agent.

Migration comes from the destination vendor's own team. AppFolio migrates from Rent Manager with dedicated onboarding and asks you to clean data first. If someone tells you an AI will handle it, ask specifically who reconciles the trial balance on the day of cutover, and who is liable if a security deposit lands in the wrong ledger.

The architectural split that matters — don't put the filters in LeadSimple

LeadSimple's recurring tasks have a design choice that breaks high-frequency work: to avoid a backlog, the next recurring task is only created once the current one is marked complete. Miss a Monday filter task and the series stalls until someone closes it. Across 300 units on a weekly cadence, that is a guaranteed pile-up.

Rent Manager's Task Automation, by contrast, posts recurring service issues without user intervention — it scans the database, finds what's due, and posts it regardless of what anyone completed. So: high-frequency, mechanical, calendar-driven work goes in Rent Manager. Low-frequency, judgment-heavy, multi-step processes go in LeadSimple. Filters and hose bibs in one; collections, renewals and onboarding in the other. Getting this backwards is the most likely way this build fails.

The pattern worth naming

Everything that landed is infrastructure. Everything that didn't is the part that makes money. The project never got past the data-loading layer: the transcript cycles through full rebuilds, clean-slate SQL, column-mapping failures, files too large, duplicate rows, and "let's recreate everything, please give me step by step." Fourteen months later the database is ready and not one email has been sent by it.

That is not a discipline problem, it is a sequencing problem — you chose a project whose first milestone was building a data pipeline from six government APIs before it could produce a single lead. There was no cheap intermediate win to sustain it.

Why this matters for the ops project

Your two projects are the same shape: recurring, data-driven actions fired off a registry of buildings. One stalled and one hasn't started. The difference is decisive, and it is the reason to be optimistic:

  • Lead generation required you to build the building registry from scratch. That's why it stalled.
  • Operations automation does not — Rent Manager already holds every building, unit and resident. You are adding fields to existing records, not assembling a database from six APIs.

So the ops work will succeed where lead-gen stalled, and it should go first. Its first milestone is two weeks away and free, which is exactly the property the lead-gen project lacked.

And the thing you should probably just buy

RegWatch — the compliance tool in Step 1 — aggregates NYC, NJ and CT property records from 250+ government sources, and its Max tier includes a Prospector lead finder, AI Analyst mode, and CRM/CSV export alongside the compliance calendar.

That is a meaningful overlap with the pipeline you have been hand-building for fourteen months, at $50–100/month. It will not replicate your BIN-level permit cross-referencing or the owner-portfolio graph — and it certainly won't deliver the "same owner, different managers" signal, which remains your original idea and your edge. But before you resume that build, spend $15 on a single one-time report and see how much of your own spec arrives pre-built. If it covers 70% of it, the right move is to buy the 70% and spend your build time only on the differentiated 30%. One tool would then serve both your compliance problem and your sales pipeline — which is as close as this session gets to your "as few tools as possible" goal.

What I'd do in the next two weeks

  • Days 1–3: Create the UDFs from the registry table in Rent Manager. This is configuration, not a project.
  • Days 4–10: Walk two buildings end to end and fill every field, including shutoffs, access codes and roof access. Time it, then extrapolate the rest and template what repeats.
  • Days 8–10: Configure Task Automation for the four highest-value cadences: hose bib shutoff (Oct is weeks away), roof drains, quarterly HVAC, heat-season check. Not all nineteen — four.
  • Day 11: Buy one $15 RegWatch report on your largest building. Compare it against your own lead-gen spec and against what you currently track manually.
  • Days 12–14: Send one monthly newsletter through Rent Manager mass communication. Prove the channel works before designing a content calendar around it.

Note what is absent from that list: no vendor demos, no migration, no AI pilots. Those all belong after the registry exists, because the registry is what makes every one of them evaluable.
